## Candidate records

### J1-SAT-001 — Santa María la Blanca: the route crosses the history before Santa Cruz explains it

- **Route section / location:** Pje. de Vila → Santa María la Blanca.
- **What the visitor can observe:** the church exterior and the street axis at the edge of the historic Judería; interior access is not required.
- **Narrative hook:** the Saturday walk passes a former synagogue before later entering the tourist image of Santa Cruz. In 1391, anti-Jewish violence and forced conversions preceded its transformation into a Christian church; the present interior was then radically rebuilt in the seventeenth century.
- **Concise factual basis:** Visita Sevilla identifies Santa María la Blanca as a thirteenth-century synagogue transformed into a Christian church after the 1391 massacre / forced conversions, with a major seventeenth-century Baroque reconstruction financed by Justino de Neve.
- **Legend / tradition status:** `NONE`.
- **Sources:** `S19`.
- **Robustness:** `HIGH` as walking-time story; do not require entering a church at this early hour and do not imply that the present exterior visually reveals every earlier phase.
- **Suggested duration:** `45-90s`.
- **/solve disposition:** `PRIMARY_WALKING_BRIEF`.

### J1-SAT-002 — Jardines de Murillo: this green threshold is a deliberate 1911 opening in the old city

- **Route section / location:** Santa María la Blanca → Jardines de Murillo, and again on the return from Plaza de España.
- **What the visitor can observe:** garden paths, the Alcázar-side wall, transition between dense streets and open landscaped space.
- **Narrative hook:** what feels like an old garden inherited intact from the Alcázar is also a modern urban intervention: royal orchard land was ceded to the city so Santa Cruz could be opened toward San Bernardo, and Juan Talavera shaped the new public gardens.
- **Concise factual basis:** the gardens came from the Huerta del Retiro of the Alcázar; after long requests, Alfonso XIII enabled the 1911 cession, a new Alcázar enclosure wall was built, and Juan Talavera y Heredia designed the gardens, with the surviving scheme executed in 1915 and the Murillo name adopted shortly afterward.
- **Legend / tradition status:** `NONE`.
- **Sources:** `S20`, `S28`.
- **Robustness:** `HIGH` after opening time; relies on large spatial features rather than a single object.
- **Suggested duration:** `45-90s`.
- **/solve disposition:** `PRIMARY`.

### J1-SAT-003 — Plaza de España: yesterday’s Triana material becomes state-scale scenery

- **Route section / location:** Plaza de España, after first taking in the whole composition.
- **What the visitor can observe:** exposed brick, extensive polychrome ceramics, wrought iron, benches, bridge balustrades and façades.
- **Narrative hook:** the ceramics seen in Triana on Friday are not merely a neighbourhood motif. The 1929 project deliberately mobilised brick and Seville/Triana ceramic craft to manufacture a modern regionalist image at monumental scale.
- **Concise factual basis:** institutional sources describe Aníbal González’s Plaza de España as combining exposed brick, ceramics and ironwork; current municipal material on González explicitly identifies Triana ceramics as one of the characteristic materials through which his regionalism projected a modern image of Seville.
- **Legend / tradition status:** `NONE`.
- **Sources:** `S22`, `S24`.
- **Robustness:** `HIGH`; material is ubiquitous and no exact tile is required.

### J1-SAT-009 — Doña Elvira: the “timeless” square is early-twentieth-century city-making plus a literary legend

- **Route section / location:** Plaza de Doña Elvira.
- **What the visitor can observe:** small pedestrian square, angled entrances, central planting, fountain, benches, orange trees and surrounding façades.
- **Narrative hook:** let the square feel old first, then reveal that its present form was created during the 1911–1918 remodelling of Santa Cruz. Its name is then tied by local legend to Don Gonzalo de Ulloa and Doña Elvira from Zorrilla’s fictional `Don Juan Tenorio` world.
- **Concise factual basis:** Visita Sevilla dates the square’s current creation to the 1911–1918 reurbanisation led by Juan Talavera y Heredia and explicitly labels the Don Juan house story as `leyenda popular sevillana`. Municipal material separately documents the broader twentieth-century reshaping of Santa Cruz for tourism / urban improvement.
- **Legend / tradition status:** `LOCAL_LITERARY_LEGEND — EXPLICITLY_LABEL`.
- **Sources:** `S28`, `S29`.
- **Robustness:** `HIGH`; large stable square features and a story that does not depend on one façade.

### J1-SAT-013 — Why the merchants needed the Lonja: commerce was crowding sacred space
